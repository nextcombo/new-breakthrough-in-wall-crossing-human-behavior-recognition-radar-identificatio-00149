Abstract: Wall-crossing human behavior recognition has important application potential in intelligent security and human-machine interaction. The presence of walls makes clutter suppression an indispensable step in concealed human behavior recognition. However, existing research has overlooked the impact of clutter suppression methods on wall-crossing human behavior information, resulting in incomplete Doppler characterization of target behaviors. This study systematically analyzed the suppressing characteristics of two commonly used clutter suppression methods: double pulse cancellation and average cancellation. It was found that double pulse cancellation highlights body information, while average cancellation retains trunk information, both of which have complementary characteristics in human behavior information representation. To fully exploit the complementary characteristics of these two methods' spectral graphs, a more comprehensive Doppler characterization was constructed. The research proposed an adaptive Doppler feature enhancement network (ADFE-Net): through the adaptive Doppler feature enhancement module, the spectral graphs of double pulse cancellation and average cancellation are processed, and adaptive learning frequency weighting factors are used to fuse and enhance complementary frequency components, generating Doppler enhanced spectral graphs; these spectral graphs are then input into a 3D coordinate attention-ConvMixer network for behavior recognition. Research Background and Introduction
The core idea of radar human behavior recognition is to extract behavioral information from radar echoes and generate characterization data, followed by deep learning for classification. However, in most application scenarios, the presence of stationary targets interferes with detection areas, making clutter suppression an essential step to highlight target information. The strong penetrability of ultra-wideband radar makes wall-crossing human behavior recognition a research hotspot, further enhancing the importance of clutter suppression.
Current research predominantly employs double-pulse cancellation or averaging to suppress clutter, yet both methods suffer from information loss: Double-pulse cancellation filters out micromotion scatter points, resulting in the loss of weak motion information from the torso; under averaging, the torso's reflection energy is significantly stronger than that of the limbs, making faint scattering from the limbs easily obscured. Furthermore, the wall's attenuation effect exacerbates this issue in scenarios involving wall crossing, ultimately leading to the loss of critical micromotion information from the human torso or limbs, and a complete representation of Doppler characteristics is compromised.
In response to this issue, this study thoroughly analyzed the impact of common clutter suppression methods on human behavior information and proposed an adaptive Doppler feature enhancement method suitable for wall-crossing human behavior recognition based on its characteristics. By integrating complementary features from two clutter suppression methods, a more comprehensive Doppler representation is constructed, achieving high-precision wall-crossing human behavior recognition.
Research Methodology Introduction: The core of this study is to propose the Adaptive Doppler Feature Enhancing Network (ADFE-Net). The overall scheme revolves around radar echo modeling analysis, clutter suppression characteristics research, feature enhancing module design, classification network construction, and experimental verification. The core methods can be divided into three key parts:
1. The modeling of wall-penetrating radar echoes and the analysis of clutter suppression characteristics are conducted using ultra-wideband step-frequency continuous wave (SFCW) radar to collect echo data from human body behaviors penetrating through walls. A model for echoes is established, which includes scattered points of the human body and direct clutter from the wall. Short-time Fourier Transform (STFT) is used to extract the time-frequency spectrum of the echoes.
System analysis of dual-pulse cancellation and average cancellation in terms of amplitude frequency characteristics: Dual-pulse cancellation has a significant suppression effect on zero frequency and low frequency bands, effectively retaining high-frequency information of limb movement; Average cancellation only strongly suppresses the zero frequency component, effectively retaining low frequency trunk micro-movement information, forming a natural complementary relationship in human behavior information representation. Taking "punching" as an example, trunk movement concentrates in the medium and low frequency area near zero frequency, while limb movement is distributed in the medium and high frequency area, both methods lose key information that the other can retain.
2. The Adaptive Doppler Feature Enhancing Module (ADFEM) is designed to integrate complementary frequency information from dual-pulse cancellation and average cancellation spectra, with the core steps being:
① Deep Convolutional Unit (DCU) performs shallow feature extraction on each spectrum separately, preserving independent features across channels;
② Combining adaptive max pooling and average pooling to extract global and local information, generating shallow frequency feature vectors through Point Convolution Unit (PCU);
③ Interactive module realizes the exchange of information between the two spectral feature sets to avoid redundant feature generation;
④ Blending independent shallow features with interactive information, generating frequency weighted factors through Sigmoid activation, weighting the input spectra before fusion, producing a Doppler enhanced spectrum, while retaining key limb and trunk movement information.
3. The 3D Coordinate Attention-ConvMixer (3DCA-ConvMixer) classification network is constructed on the basis of feature enhancement, building a classification network that combines low temporal-spatial complexity with high recognition accuracy:
① Patch Embedding is used to divide the enhanced spectrum into non-overlapping blocks, achieving local feature aggregation and long-distance information interaction.
② Stack DCU, 3DCA modules and PCU to form full convolutional blocks, extracting deep features; wherein the 3DCA module extends lightweight efficient channel attention (ECA) to time, frequency, channel three dimensions, achieving selective enhancement of three-dimensional features and reducing irrelevant information.
③ Introduce residual structure to alleviate performance degradation caused by increasing network depth, ultimately achieving precise classification of human behaviors.
Additionally, a dedicated dataset for wall-crossing human behavior was constructed. Using a UWB SFCW radar with one transmitter and one receiver, data were collected from 10 subjects at a thickness of 24cm in a wall, covering 11 types of human behaviors (such as punching, drinking water, falling, stepping, etc.), totaling 1650 samples. Multi-dimensional evaluation indicators including macro average accuracy, precision, F1 score, computational complexity (FLOPs), and parameter count were set to ensure the comprehensiveness of experimental verification.
Research conclusion: This study addresses the issue of Doppler frequency information being incomplete due to clutter suppression in existing wall-crossing human behavior recognition by proposing a method based on Doppler feature enhancement for wall-crossing human behavior recognition. The core research findings are as follows:
1. 
For the first time, this study systematically analyzes the amplitude-frequency characteristics and their impact on human behavior information of two commonly used clutter suppression methods: double pulse cancellation and mean cancellation. It clarifies the complementary nature of these methods in high-frequency limb information and low-frequency trunk information representation, providing a theoretical basis for the selection of clutter suppression methods in wall-crossing human behavior recognition.
2. 
The designed ADFEM module can adaptively fuse complementary frequency components from both double pulse cancellation and mean cancellation spectra, generating a more comprehensive Doppler enhanced spectrum graph that fully retains the micromotion information of the body and limbs, solving the problem of information loss caused by single clutter suppression method.
3. 
The 3DCA-ConvMixer classification network, while preserving the long-range information capture capability of ConvMixer, achieves time, frequency, and channel three-dimensional feature selection through the 3DCA module, balancing recognition accuracy with computational efficiency.
The experimental results show that the accuracy of the proposed ADFE-Net network for wall-crossing human behavior recognition reaches 97.57%, which is at least 4.24% higher than existing methods; meanwhile, this method only introduces minimal computational complexity and parameter increase, demonstrating superior performance in scenarios with limited training samples and varying signal-to-noise ratios, possessing good robustness.
Furthermore, the study clarifies the applicable scenarios for two methods of clutter suppression: when behavioral differentiation features are primarily based on limb movements, a double-pulse cancellation spectrum can effectively identify; when trunk movement is predominant, the representation effect of an average cancellation spectrum is superior; for behaviors that require integration of trunk and limb features, the comprehensive integration of the two spectrums can achieve the most complete behavioral representation.
This study provides a new technical approach for wall-crossing radar human behavior recognition. The proposed feature fusion and enhancement methods also offer references for radar micro Doppler signal processing, concealed target identification, and other related fields. They hold significant engineering application value in intelligent security, counter-terrorism operations, indoor human-computer interaction, etc.
